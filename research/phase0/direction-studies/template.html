Three ways to hold five thousand years

Each frame below renders the same real moment, the world of 117 CE, in a different visual language. The borders, the strata beneath Rome and the streams of land are computed from Cliopatria, the open polity atlas CHRONOS will be built on. It is one atlas's interpretation, and it is far from complete: in 117 CE it maps polities over 15.7% of the world's ice-free land. Drag any globe. Hover or tap a territory.

    Recommendation

    Build Strata

    It is the only direction where the interface itself answers the brief's hardest questions: who ruled here, who coexisted, and who held the most land, using a measure we can defend.

    Why

    The core sample answers "who ruled this ground?" in one click. The time stream answers "who was contemporary, and who was largest?" with mapped extent from real polygons, never an invented power score. Gaps, near-misses and overlaps are drawn from day one. The validation findings have a place in the layout, too: flagged cross-references, the coverage statement and the data trace. Honesty becomes part of the look instead of a footnote.

    Borrow from Nocturne

    The stage. A night-side globe with an atmospheric rim for the opening shot and for zooming out into deep time.

    Borrow from Vellum

    The lettering. Letter-spaced names set along each territory's spine, and a paper print skin for classroom handouts later.

    Watch

    Strata puts more on screen. On phones the globe comes first, the core sample becomes a bottom sheet and the stream opens on demand.
